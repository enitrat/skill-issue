# Sources behind each principle

Where each section of SKILL.md comes from. Quotes were read from the primary page unless marked "secondhand". Go here to deepen a principle or to cite it.

## 1. Know what you think before you arrange it
- Paul Graham, "Putting Ideas into Words" — https://paulgraham.com/words.html — "Ideas can feel complete. It's only when you try to put them into words that you discover they're not."
- Paul Graham, "Writing, Briefly" — https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html — write a bad version fast, rewrite, cut.
- Henrik Karlsson's editor, via sankalp — https://x.com/dejavucoder/status/2063578308613472735 — ask "why do you care?" until the honest answer appears, then write that.
- William Fitzpatrick — https://x.com/WriterScience/status/2063585268847001946 — "A subject isn't an idea; it's just a category."
- Bezos on narratives (relayed) — https://www.anecdote.com/2018/05/amazons-six-page-narrative-structure/ — narratives force the writer to decide what matters more than what.
- Will Larson on strategy writing (secondhand) — the order you write is not the order the reader should read.

## 2. Pose the question before you answer it
- Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*, via Will Larson — https://lethain.com/pyramid-principle/ — situation, complication, question, answer; the situation tells the reader what they already know.
- BLUF — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLUF_(communication) and Matt Strom — https://mattstromawn.com/writing/bluf — the answer first when the reader holds the question; Strom and the Air Force *Tongue and Quill* note that skeptical readers may stop after it, and that BLUF is "not a formula".
- SBAR — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBAR — situation, background, assessment, recommendation, for handovers.
- Patrick Winston, "How to Speak" (MIT), via Lumen — start with an empathetic connection, not the conclusion.

## 3. Make each part answer the question the previous part raised
- Minto via Larson — "Any point you make must raise a question in the reader's mind, which you must answer horizontally on the line below."
- Bezos (relayed) — "on page 2 you have a question but on page 4 that question is answered."
- McKinsey-style action titles and the "read only the titles" test (secondhand: practitioner guides on slide writing).
- Tufte on the Columbia slide — https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/columbia-accident-investigation-board-the-boeing-powerpoint-slide/ — bullets "show effects without causes".
- Nygard, ADRs — full sentences, not fragments in bullets.

## 4. Order information so understanding builds
- George Gopen and Judith Swan, "The Science of Scientific Writing" (1990) — via https://wstomv.win.tue.nl/quotes/science-of-scientific-writing.html — topic position (old information) at the start, stress position (new information) at the end; "Information is interpreted more easily and more uniformly if it is placed where most readers expect to find it."
- Joseph Williams, *Style* (secondhand) — characters as subjects, actions as verbs; old before new.
- Bret Victor, "Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction" — https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/
- Bartosz Ciechanowski — https://ciechanow.ski/lights-and-shadows/
- Rust RFC template (guide-level before reference-level) — https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md
- React RFC template (motivation that survives rejection) — https://github.com/reactjs/rfcs/blob/main/0000-template.md
- TC39 process — https://tc39.es/process-document/ — the problem first, then illustrative usage, then the specification.
- Richard Rumelt via Will Larson (secondhand) — strategy starts from diagnosis; failed strategies usually had a lazy diagnosis.
- Anthropic, Fable 5.2 prompting guide — no undefined reference in line 1 that makes sense only at line 12.

## 5. Spend words where the reasoning is
- Diátaxis — https://diataxis.fr/ — four kinds of documentation for four needs; mixing them "fails both audiences".
- Rust RFC "Rationale and alternatives"; Ubl, "Design docs at Google" — https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/ — the trade-offs are the document's long-term value.
- Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" — https://www.orwell.ru/library/essays/politics/english/e_polit — "If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out."
- Polylane — https://polylane.com/blog/nobody-reads-pull-request-descriptions-so-we-made-videos/ — a 565-word description for a 58-line diff that nobody read.
- emo.eth — https://x.com/emo_eth/status/2089782739960783265 — agents "writing text inside css flexboxes and not visualizing anything".
- Anthropic, Fable 5.2 prompting guide — hard sentence-length rules make prose choppy.

## 6. Earn trust
- Edward Tufte — "compared to what?"; the Columbia analysis on dequantification and the buried warning — https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/powerpoint-does-rocket-science-and-better-techniques-for-technical-reports/
- Hugo Richard — http://hugorcd.com/writing/what-my-pull-requests-look-like-now — evidence matched to the kind of change; say what was not checked.
- Rust RFC "Drawbacks"; PEP 1 "Rejected Ideas" — https://peps.python.org/pep-0001/ ; Google design docs "Alternatives considered"; Nygard ADR negative consequences.
- Amazon writing tips (circulating, provenance unverified) — data instead of adjectives, "87%" instead of "most".

## 7. Write for someone who is not you
- Steven Pinker on the curse of knowledge — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/the-curse-of-knowledge-pinker-describes-a-key-cause-of-bad-writing — "The more you know, the less clearly you write."
- Anthropic, Fable 5.2 prompting guide — "Write for an intelligent but low-context reader"; no coined terms.
- Orwell, rules 2, 4 and 5 — short words, active voice, no jargon where an everyday word exists.
- Gergely Orosz — https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/becoming-a-better-writer — edit from the reader's side.

## 8. Reread as the reader
- Paul Graham — "pretend to be a neutral reader who knows nothing of what's in your head, only what you wrote."
- Pinker — test the draft on someone like the reader.
- Hugo Richard — end with two or three plain sentences; if they do not make sense, the change probably does not either.
- Mike Taylor, blind test of writing skills — https://x.com/hammer_mt/status/2119906374066835495 — a writing skill helps only once it has been tested against real output.
